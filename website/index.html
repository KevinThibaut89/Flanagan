A personal mixology app

Your bar, and what it can make tonight.

Flanagan keeps track of your bottles and your everyday staples — the limes, the syrup, the soda — and tells you honestly which cocktails are within reach. Not ten thousand recipes. The ones you can pour.

The tonight deck

Only what your shelf can actually make.

Every suggestion is checked against your inventory before it reaches you — bottle by bottle, staple by staple. A drink you can’t pour is worse than one fewer suggestion, so Flanagan simply doesn’t show it.

And when you’re close, it says so. The one-bottle-away ledger ranks which single purchase would unlock the most new drinks — so the next bottle you buy is the one that earns its shelf space.

Ask the barkeep

“Something bitter and stirred.”

Describe what you feel like, in your own words. The barkeep answers in plain language — and only from what’s on your shelf. No suggestions for bottles you don’t own; no shopping list disguised as a recipe.

  • Something fresh and citrusy
  • Short, strong and spirit-forward
  • Use up my rum
  • Surprise me

The shelf

Your bar, kept without the bookkeeping.

Every bottle carries a fill gauge and a category; a swipe marks one empty when it runs dry. Staples — the limes, the sugar, the soda water — live alongside, because they decide whether a drink is actually possible.

Adding is the fast part. Scan a barcode, or point the camera at the whole shelf and let Flanagan do the counting — it reads the labels, you tick what to keep.

The notebook

Every recipe, in one hand.

Suggestions you save and recipes you write yourself land in the same notebook, in the same format, numbered like the pages of a menu. Photograph a page from a book, a menu, a screenshot — Flanagan reads it and fills the editor for you.

Millilitres or ounces, at a toggle. A dash stays a dash.
